# Phase 0 — Market Validation

## Objective

Determine whether customers will publish repair problems and whether providers will respond to qualified local requests before investing in backend infrastructure.

## Primary market

Initial geography: Riga and nearby municipalities.

Initial verticals:

1. Computers and IT
2. Phones and tablets
3. Home repairs
4. Appliances
5. Automotive
6. Property maintenance

The first validation cycle should not attempt equal coverage. Compare verticals and identify where urgency, ticket value, fragmentation and provider acquisition produce the strongest marketplace liquidity.

## Core hypotheses

### H1 — Customer problem

Customers experience high search cost, unclear provider quality, slow responses and difficulty comparing prices.

Evidence required:

- Customers describe a recent repair search without prompting.
- At least 30% of interviewed customers say they would submit a structured request for a similar problem.
- At least ten complete the prototype wizard without assistance.

### H2 — Provider problem

Independent specialists need more qualified leads but dislike broad, low-intent advertising and irrelevant enquiries.

Evidence required:

- Providers confirm unused capacity or inconsistent lead flow.
- At least five agree to join a manual pilot.
- At least three state a plausible willingness to pay for qualified leads or a subscription.

### H3 — Marketplace mechanism

Structured requests and comparable offers create more trust and decision value than a plain provider directory.

Evidence required:

- Users can explain why they would choose one offer over another.
- At least 70% identify price, arrival, warranty, rating or distance as useful comparison fields.
- Fewer than 20% require immediate direct calling as the only acceptable workflow.

| Segment | Target | Minimum |

| Recent repair customers | 25 | 20 |

| Pilot-ready providers | 8 | 5 |

## Customer interview script

1. Tell me about the last item or property problem you needed repaired.
2. How did you find a specialist?
3. What took the most time or created uncertainty?
4. How many providers did you contact?
5. What information did you need before choosing?
6. What stopped you from choosing faster?
7. Show the prototype without explaining it. Ask the participant to publish the same problem.
8. Ask what they expect to happen after publishing.
9. Ask which offer they would choose and why.
10. Ask whether they would use this for their next repair and what would prevent them.

Avoid asking whether the idea is “good.” Ask about past behaviour, observed friction and concrete next-use intent.

## Provider interview script

1. Which repair categories and geographic areas do you serve?
2. Where do new jobs currently come from?
3. Which leads waste the most time?
4. What information is normally missing before you can estimate the work?
5. How quickly can you respond to a qualified request?
6. What percentage of enquiries become paid work?
7. Review the provider dashboard and one sample request.
8. What would make you respond or ignore this job?
9. Would you pay per qualified lead, per accepted job or monthly? Why?
10. What verification or marketplace rules would be mandatory?

## Landing-page experiment

Traffic sources:

- Local Facebook groups
- LinkedIn provider outreach
- Direct messages to repair businesses
- QR code during in-person interviews
- Small paid search test only after organic messaging is stable

Primary conversion events:

- Start request
- Complete request
- Open provider demo
- Submit feedback
- Join pilot manually through a linked form or email

## Decision thresholds

Proceed to Phase 2 only when all minimum conditions are met:

- 20 customer interviews completed
- 15 provider interviews completed
- 15 observed prototype sessions
- 30% or higher credible customer next-use intent
- 5 providers agree to a pilot
- One vertical demonstrates plausible local liquidity
- No unresolved trust or legal issue invalidates the workflow

Pause or narrow scope when:

- Customer demand exists but providers will not respond quickly enough
- Providers want leads but customers strongly prefer direct directories
- The chosen vertical has insufficient request frequency
- Acquisition cost assumptions exceed realistic transaction value
